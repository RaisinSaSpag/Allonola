Decision Tree

"Will this order be late or returned, and why?"

What it is

A Decision Tree is a flowchart of yes/no questions—for example, "Is the seller rating below 3.5? Is the shipping distance long?"—that branches systematically down to a clear operational prediction such as "likely late".

Why Allonola uses it

Allonola does not operate its own fulfillment trucks; third-party couriers carry orders from independent sellers directly to buyers. When packages are delayed or returned, support staff must know the exact root cause to coordinate constructively with couriers and merchants. Transparent decision branches provide full auditability, advancing Operational Goal #4 (reach 92% on-time delivery) and Operational Goal #5 (keep return rate below 6% of orders).

How it works

The algorithm evaluates candidate features and selects the question split that best separates outcomes by minimizing impurity. Each branch represents a human-readable condition, and each leaf node produces a final prediction with associated confidence.

Gini Impurity: Gini(D) = 1 - ∑ [p_i]² Information Gain: Gain(D, Feature) = Entropy(D) - ∑ (|D_v| / |D|) × Entropy(D_v)

The primary strength of decision trees is total explainability: staff can inspect the exact sequence of thresholds that triggered an operational warning. Overfitting is prevented by limiting maximum tree depth and pruning branches with minimal sample support.

Interpretation

            

              Late precision is 0.48 (119 of 249 late warnings correct); late recall is 0.61 (119 of 194 late orders caught). The tree is shallow (depth 4) so staff can read every rule; its warnings are about half false alarms, and the trade-off is set by the training choices in the pipeline.
            

          


          

            
Business Impact

            

              Operations staff would review 249 flagged orders out of 600 and catch 119 of 194 delays (61%) before dispatch. The rule trails help root-cause conversations with couriers and sellers.
            

          

        

      


      
      

        
Strengths and limitations

        

          

            
Strengths

            
    
              
  • Completely transparent and easy for non-technical staff to audit.
    • 
              
  • Generates natural, plain-English operational decision trails.
    • 
              
  • Requires no feature normalization or standard scaling.
    • 
              
  • Handles mixed categorical and continuous numerical features naturally.
    • 
            

          


          

            
Limitations

            
    
              
  • Can quickly overfit and memorize training noise if tree depth is unconstrained.
    • 
              
  • Axis-aligned orthogonal splits struggle with diagonal linear feature combinations.
    • 
              
  • Small changes or revisions in the training dataset can alter the entire tree layout.
    • 
              
  • Can be biased toward features with more unique categories or levels.
    •
