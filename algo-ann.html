Artificial Neural Network (ANN)

"What will this shopper browse, and are they at risk of leaving?"

What it is

An Artificial Neural Network is a computational program loosely inspired by the biological brain, made of connected layers of small mathematical units that learn complicated browsing habits, such as what a shopper browses before deciding to purchase.

Why Allonola uses it

Retaining existing customers is far more cost-effective than acquiring new shoppers through advertising. When a loyal customer stops visiting or displays declining engagement, deep sequential neural networks detect subtle churn risk patterns weeks before they lapse. This capability enables our marketing team to dispatch targeted re-engagement offers, directly supporting Operational Goal #6: raising the marketplace repeat-buyer rate from 28% to 35%.

How it works

Information flows from an input layer through one or more hidden layers to an output prediction. Each neuron multiplies incoming inputs by learned weights, sums them with a bias, and applies an activation function (such as ReLU). Training iteratively minimizes prediction errors using backpropagation and gradient descent.

Neuron Activation: a^[l] = ReLU(W^[l] · a^[l-1] + b^[l]) Binary Cross-Entropy Loss: L = - [ y · log(ŷ) + (1 - y) · log(1 - ŷ) ] Weight Gradient Update: W^[l] := W^[l] - α (∂L / ∂W^[l])

Dense learned vector embeddings represent user browsing sequences and product identities in a shared semantic space. Dropout and early stopping are essential safeguards: because neural networks possess immense capacity, early stopping monitors validation loss to halt training the moment generalization ceases to improve.

Interpretation

An AUC of 0.91 means a random churned shopper ranks above a random active shopper 91% of the time. The widening gap after epoch 22 signals memorization of training clickstreams, which early stopping prevents in production by halting optimization.

Business Impact

Retention offers are targeted precisely at the top 5% of churn-risk shoppers, measured against an untreated control group. S-01's personalized homepage led by running shoes and a phone stand produced an add-to-cart conversion the next day, supporting the corporate target of raising repeat buyers from 28% to 35%.

Strengths and limitations

Strengths

  • Highest potential predictive accuracy across massive consumer behavioral datasets.
  • Naturally models intricate non-linear relationships and dense latent embeddings.
  • Supports multi-task learning (jointly predicting churn probability and item rankings).
  • Ingests multimodal inputs, from click timestamps to product image vectors.

Limitations

  • Requires extensive sample sizes and substantial GPU computing resources.
  • Difficult to explain to merchants why an item received a specific ranking score.
  • Prone to overfitting and parameter memorization without early stopping and dropout.
  • Significant engineering overhead to deploy low-latency inference at the edge.
