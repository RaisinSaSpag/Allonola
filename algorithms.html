Algorithms Architecture

Six machine learning methods, each answering a different business question.

Explore how Allonola matches each operational challenge to the appropriate statistical and machine learning technique, balancing speed, explainability, and predictive depth.

Which question does each one answer?

Each method is a different kind of helper. Pick the question you care about to read its plain-language guide.

  • How many units will sell?Estimates sales for a price and promotion. Used by sellers to plan prices and stock.
  • Is this review real?Sorts reviews into positive, negative, or spam so buyers see honest feedback.
  • What should I look at next?Suggests products that shoppers with similar tastes bought.
  • Is this order risky?Flags unusual orders so a person can check them first.
  • Will this delivery be late?Uses a checklist of simple questions to warn about late deliveries.
  • Is this shopper about to leave?Estimates how likely a shopper is to stop using Allonola.

Choosing a method

When predicting a numerical target like weekly sales volume, Linear Regression provides immediate, transparent price-elasticity estimates.

When grouping text or categorizing orders where operations staff must understand the exact reasoning, Decision Trees and Naive Bayes deliver fast, explainable rules.

When discovering subtle browsing patterns across millions of item interactions, high-capacity models like K-Nearest Neighbors and Artificial Neural Networks maximize recommendation relevance where human auditing is secondary to personal affinity.
